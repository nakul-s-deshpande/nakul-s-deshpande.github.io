Creep

People have wondered for over a hundred years how soil moves downhill on slopes too gentle to slide.1 The standing explanation is disturbance: gophers, frost, rain, roots, all churning the ground. So we built a hillslope in the lab where we could switch the disturbances off. It crept anyway.2 It is still, as far as we can tell, creeping.

Fig. 1. A sandpile, left alone in a quiet lab, keeps creeping, indefinitely, at rates much like real hillslopes. It slows as it settles. A change in temperature or humidity wakes it up again. Shaking it, oddly, does the opposite. Fig. 2. Different grains, different walls, rougher and smoother. The creep didn't much care. The details did. Fig. 3. Twenty-seven authors and one question: what kind of material is the ground?
